06 Selected Projects Bigger pieces, full notes

The long form.

A few projects worth dwelling on — solo shows, client work, jams, and long-running research.

Spring 2026
NYU Shanghai

Between Waves

Solo exhibition · multimedia interactive

Solo exhibition of interactive multimedia artwork at NYU Shanghai. Conceived, fabricated, and installed end to end — combining physical media, video, and interactive components.

2026
Design Thinking

HappyPower — "Biking and Blooming" V3

Product redesign · proposal accepted, advancing to next stage

Redesigned a Chinese cycling product from a passive light installation into a navigation-and-charging system for urban cyclists. Researched MEMS mirror, supercapacitor storage, and Class 2 laser optics; wrote the full app interaction flow and the safety architecture (tilt shut-off, lane-boundary projection). Proposal accepted by the client; moving into next development stage.

Spring 2026
Experience Studio

Wild China Education — Experiential Module

Project lead · client-facing concept and delivery

Designed an experiential business module for international university students on how design drives virality and consumer culture in China. Ran logistics, scheduling, and delivery across a multi-week timeline; tested the experience on incoming students during NYU Shanghai Admit Weekend; handed off a facilitation guide, interactive artifacts, and a creative media teaser to Wild China Education / Beyond Classrooms.

2025
48-hour jam

Endless Studio Game Jam

Art direction · 3D environment · Unity build · featured entry

A short atmospheric exploration game built with a small team over a weekend. Took the lead on visual direction and 3D environment art in Blender, brought assets into Unity, and handled the sound design pass. Selected among the jam's featured entries.

2019—2022
Comilla University

Heritage Digitization — Bengal Trade Sites

Archival research · visual documentation · 2 peer-reviewed papers

Long-running collaboration mapping and transcribing historical trade sites across Bengal. Sketches, field notes, and archival structuring fed into museum and exhibition use, and supported two peer-reviewed papers on archaeotourism and early trade.
